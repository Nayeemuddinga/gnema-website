G-NeMa Frontier Research Dossier

From frontier concept to engineering discipline.

This dossier separates scientific foundations, observed research progress, engineering architecture and commercialization considerations. Commercial-readiness statements are G-NeMa's analytical framing, not guarantees of market adoption.

01 · EXECUTIVE BRIEF
The agency economy describes an operating model in which software agents become active participants in workflows: discovering tasks, negotiating bounded responsibilities, calling services, coordinating with other agents and producing auditable outcomes. It should be treated as an architectural and governance concept rather than a claim that software will replace all human work.
02 · SCIENTIFIC FOUNDATIONS

The scientific foundations combine multi-agent systems, distributed systems, mechanism design, reinforcement learning, language-model planning, identity and authorization. Agents require explicit goals, capabilities, communication protocols, state, memory and mechanisms for conflict resolution. Economic coordination adds pricing, service-level agreements, reputation, incentives and machine-readable contracts.

03 · REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE
Human intent → task market/orchestrator → agent identity & capability registry → negotiation/protocol layer → specialized agents → enterprise tools/APIs → verification → settlement/outcome ledger → human escalation. A trust fabric sits across the stack with authentication, authorization, policy and provenance.
04 · CURRENT STATE

Enterprise multi-agent prototypes are expanding, while research is actively studying coordination, security and transparency. The practical near-term market is more likely to be agent-mediated business processes than a fully autonomous economy. Cross-organization agent interaction remains especially immature because trust, identity, liability and data-sharing rules are unresolved.

05 · COMMERCIAL READINESS

Medium for internal enterprise orchestration and bounded digital work; low-to-medium for open cross-company agent markets. Commercial readiness rises sharply when the workflow has standardized APIs, clear ownership and reversible transactions.

06 · G-NeMa RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions worth engineering.

These questions define potential G-NeMa research programs, prototypes and architecture experiments. They are intentionally framed as open problems rather than product claims.

  1. What should an enterprise-grade agent identity and reputation system contain?
  2. How can agents negotiate without exposing sensitive internal state?
  3. How should agent outcomes be priced when work is probabilistic?
  4. Can G-NeMa define a reference architecture for an enterprise agent marketplace?

G-NeMa architecture view

Intelligence is a stack, not a single model.

Our research connects computation, data, models, agents, interfaces, physical systems and governance. Each frontier becomes useful when its components can operate together under explicit constraints and observable outcomes.

For enterprise and mission environments, the design target is controlled capability: measurable performance, traceability, resilience and clear human authority.
